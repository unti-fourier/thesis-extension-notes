# Amenability descends over countable compact Hausdorff Glimm fibres

**Work in progress.** Written verification only; not a paper, preprint, Lean formalization, or claim of priority.

Let \(A\ne0\) be a separable CCR algebra, \(S=\operatorname{Prim}(A)\), and \(Y=\operatorname{Glimm}(A)\) compact metrizable, with Glimm map \(p:S\to Y\). Let a discrete group \(\Gamma\) act on \(A\). Suppose every fibre \(F_y=p^{-1}(y)\) is countable and compact Hausdorff **in its relative topology in \(S\)**. A central equivariant mean \(\ell^\infty(\Gamma,Z(A^{**}))\to Z(A^{**})\) then implies that \(\Gamma\curvearrowright Y\) is topologically amenable. No uniform Cantor–Bendixson bound needs to be assumed.

The new step beyond the [finite-fibre argument](01-finite-glimm-fibre-descent.md) is to construct an equivariant Borel probability kernel on \(p\). A countable compact Hausdorff fibre is metrizable by second countability, and zero-dimensional: distance from a fixed point takes a countable set of values, so radii avoiding these values give clopen neighborhoods. Its final nonempty Cantor–Bendixson derivative is finite. Use the uniform probability on that derivative. This choice is preserved by every homeomorphism of fibres. What needs proof is its Borel dependence on \(y\).

Take a single countable open basis on \(S\), and list all its finite unions, including the empty union. For each pair \(U,V\) on the list, the condition on \(y\) that \(U\cap F_y,V\cap F_y\) be disjoint and cover \(F_y\) is Borel. Failure is the projection of a Borel set with countable sections (use that \(S\) is standard Borel for separable CCR, as in the finite-fibre note); apply Lusin–Novikov uniformization. When the test succeeds, assign to \(s\in F_y\) the coordinate \(1_U(s)\) for that pair; otherwise use zero. The resulting map \(s\mapsto (p(s),\iota(s))\) into \(Y\times 2^{\mathbb N}\) is Borel. On each fibre the coordinates are continuous and separate points: any clopen subset and its complement are both compact, so their basis covers have finite subcovers. Thus each fibre embeds homeomorphically as a compact subset \(K_y\) of \(2^{\mathbb N}\).

By Lusin–Souslin the graph of \(y\mapsto K_y\) is Borel. It has countable sections, so for each basic open \(O\subseteq 2^{\mathbb N}\), the set of \(y\) with \(K_y\cap O\ne\varnothing\) is Borel by Lusin–Novikov. These hit tests generate the hyperspace Borel structure, hence the family \(y\mapsto K_y\) is a Borel map into the compact hyperspace. Its range is analytic and consists of countable compacts. The boundedness theorem for the Cantor–Bendixson coanalytic rank gives a common countable upper bound on the first empty derivative. See [Kechris (1995), Classical Descriptive Set Theory](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4), §§18, 34–35.

At every fixed stage below this bound the derivative has Borel graph. Alternatively, at a successor stage one can test, in each basic neighborhood of a point in the previous stage, for a distinct point of that stage using the same countable-section projection theorem; limits are countable intersections. Compactness implies a first empty derivative cannot occur at a limit stage; the preceding stage is compact discrete and therefore finite. Countably many Borel tests choose that last nonempty stage, giving a Borel finite-section graph \(L\subseteq S\) in the original fibres. Lusin–Novikov again gives Borel counting on \(L_y\), so \(k_y(E)=|E\cap L_y|/|L_y|\) is a Borel probability kernel. Since \(\Gamma\) maps fibres homeomorphically, \(k_{gy}=g_*k_y\).

The [prior descent proof](01-finite-glimm-fibre-descent.md), Sections 2–4, uses finiteness only to supply such a kernel. Lifting measures by \(k_y\), then taking the adjoint through the central predual identification, gives a normal positive unital equivariant retraction onto \(C(Y)^{**}\). Composing with the central mean and applying the same Reiter argument proves the conclusion.

The common countable basis is doing real work. A bare Borel map with countable fibres, equipped separately with arbitrary compact topologies on the fibres, is insufficient: on a fixed countable label set one can switch which of two labels is the unique accumulation point according to a non-Borel subset of the base. Even rank one then fails to give a Borel choice. Here the relative topology and its common countable basis exclude that defect.
