# An Euler class for a sphere action without invariant probability

**Work in progress.** This is a written-checked research note, not a paper, a preprint, a Lean formalization, or a claim of priority.

Let \(G=\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb R)\) act on \(X=S^2\) by \(g\cdot x=gx/\|gx\|\). Write \(m\) for round probability on \(X\), which is quasi-invariant. For the Bochner crossed Banach algebra \(L^1(G,C(X);\alpha)\) and for the corresponding row-norm completion, there is an absolute Hochschild 2-class of seminorm \(1/2\) with coefficients in \(\mathcal B(C(X),L^\infty(X,m;\mathbb C))\), with the bimodule structure of the [split comparison](02-locally-compact-absolute-hh2-split-injection.md), Sections 3–4.

The action is transitive. The stabilizer \(H\) of \(e_1\) consists of upper block-triangular matrices with diagonal blocks \((\det D)^{-1}\) and \(D\in\mathrm{GL}_2^+(\mathbb R)\). The homomorphism \(H\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\) given by \(D\mapsto D/\sqrt{\det D}\) has the section \(S\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(1,S)\). Thus \(H\) is nonamenable, and the action is not topologically amenable: topological amenability passes to restrictions to closed subgroups, and an amenable action with a fixed point forces that subgroup to be amenable. There is also no invariant probability on \(X\). Rotations force it to be \(m\), but at \(e_1\) the tangent Jacobian of the normalized action of \(\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1/2)\) is \(1/8\), not \(1\). The quotient map \(G\to X\) has no continuous section either: the second column of a section, projected to \(x^\perp\), would give a nowhere-zero tangent field on \(S^2\).

Pull back the bounded Euler class of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\) to \(H\) along the displayed homomorphism composed with the quotient by \(\{\pm I\}\). Its seminorm remains \(1/2\). The quotient has a contractive cochain left inverse obtained by averaging independently over the two lifts of each argument in the continuous homogeneous bounded complex; the split map from \(H\) has a contractive left inverse by restriction. The real scalar norm computation extends unchanged to complex coefficients by taking real parts (and using the real representative for the upper bound). For the scalar computation see [Burger, Iozzi and Wienhard (2010), Theorem 5.2 and §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.2894).

Apply the isometric bounded Shapiro isomorphism for the closed subgroup \(H\) with trivial scalar coefficients. Its induction module identifies with \(W=L^\infty(G/H,m;\mathbb C)\) with the pullback action after inversion interchanges the left- and right-coset conventions. Quasi-invariance is sufficient; an invariant probability is not needed. See [Monod (2001), Proposition 10.1.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/b80626) and the homogeneous-space discussion of [Sarti and Savini (2023 preprint), §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07765v2#S6). The induced class has seminorm \(1/2\) in \(H^2_{\mathrm{cb}}(G,W)\). The contractively split comparison (with separable predual \(L^1(X,m)\) and the multiplication action of \(C(X)\) on \(W\)) preserves that seminorm in \(HH^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(A,\mathcal B(C(X),W))\) for either choice of \(A\). No evaluation of an arbitrary \(L^\infty\) cochain at a single point is used.

The distinction from the [compact lattice quotient example](02-lattice-quotient-euler-hochschild.md) is that integration against \(m\) is not an equivariant retraction to the scalars. The lower bound instead comes from the closed stabilizer via bounded induction. This is one example, not a converse for all nonamenable locally compact actions.
