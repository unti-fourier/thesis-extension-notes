# Which part of a crossed product acts on right-\(\ell^1\) coefficients?

**Work in progress, November 2026.** Research notes toward a possible future paper. This is not a submission, a peer-reviewed publication, or a publication-list entry. The proof has been independently checked in writing; no Lean verification or priority claim is made.

Let a discrete group \(\Gamma\) act on a nonempty compact Hausdorff space \(X\). Write \(B_\mu=C(X)\rtimes_\mu\Gamma\) for either the full or reduced crossed product and \(F=X^\Gamma\) for the common fixed-point set. Consider contractive Banach \(B_\mu\)-bimodules \(E\) satisfying
\[
\sum_i\|\xi f_i\|\le \|\xi\|\,\Big\|\sum_i f_i\Big\|_\infty
\]
for nonnegative \(f_i\in C(X)\) and \(\xi\in E\). No unitality assumption on \(E\) is needed.

**Claim.** The common right annihilator of these modules is the closed two-sided ideal \(J_\mu\) generated by \(C_0(X\setminus F)\), and
\[
B_\mu/J_\mu\cong C(F,C^*_\mu(\Gamma)).
\]
In particular, without a common fixed point all these right module actions are zero. The reduced statement does not require exactness of \(\Gamma\).

**Disjoint corners.** For \(f,h\) with disjoint supports choose \(0\le p\le1\) with \(fp=f\), \(ph=0\). Set \(\eta=\xi f\) and \(r=p+\varepsilon bh\) for \(b\in B_\mu\). Then \(\eta rp=\eta\), \(\eta r(1-p)=\varepsilon\xi fbh\), and \(rr^*=p^2+\varepsilon^2bhh^*b^*\). Thus
\[
\varepsilon\|\xi fbh\|
 \le \big(\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2\|bh\|^2}-1\big)\|\eta\|.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives \(E(fbh)=0\). Continuous cutoffs followed by norm limits give the same conclusion when \(fh=0\). If \(gx\ne x\), choose \(h\in C(X)\) nonzero at \(x\) and supported in a neighborhood disjoint from its \(g\)-translate. There is \(f\) with \(fh=0\) and \(fu_gh=u_gh\). Thus \(Eu_gh=0\) and \(Eh=0\), since the right annihilator is a two-sided ideal. Such \(h\) generate \(C_0(X\setminus F)\).

**Identifying the quotient.** At \(x\in F\), evaluation sends \(f\) to \(f(x)1\) and \(u_g\) to the group unitary. In the reduced case this is a component of the regular representation built from the direct sum of all point evaluations. Taken together, these maps give \(B_\mu/J_\mu\to C(F,C_\mu^*(\Gamma))\). Conversely, in the quotient the images of \(C(F)\) and the canonical \(C_\mu^*(\Gamma)\) commute and generate it. Their product gives a map from the maximal tensor product, which is the spatial tensor product because \(C(F)\) is nuclear. Fixed-point evaluation is inverse on elementary tensors, and the product map is onto. Each fixed point then gives a contractive bimodule by pulling back the regular multiplication module of \(C_\mu^*(\Gamma)\); the right \(C(X)\)-action is scalar there. These modules detect every nonzero element of the quotient. If \(F=\varnothing\), the ideal contains \(1\) and the quotient is zero.

**Contrast with the row-norm algebra.** This collapse does not carry to \(A_0(\Gamma,X)\) with its row norm, as in [Awazu (2025), Definitions 2.3 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08357). On its regular dual, \((\varphi\cdot a)(c)=\varphi(ac)\). Left multiplication by \(f_i\) acts on every coefficient by the same function \(f_i\); consequently \(\|\sum_i f_i a_i\|_{\rm row}\le\|\sum_i f_i\|_\infty\) for \(\|a_i\|_{\rm row}\le1\). Choosing scalar phases independently proves the right-\(\ell^1\) estimate. Evaluating \(\varphi\cdot a\) at \(1\) detects every nonzero \(a\), so the common right annihilator for the row-norm algebra is zero.

## Consequence for derivations

For either crossed product, every bounded derivation into the dual of every such coefficient module is inner if and only if the fixed-point quotient \(Q=B_\mu/J_\mu\) is amenable as a Banach algebra. These statements are equivalent to \(F=\varnothing\) or \(\Gamma\) being amenable. The criterion is unchanged if one tests only bimodules essential on both sides.

To see the descent, write \(B=B_\mu\), \(J=J_\mu\), and \(V=E^*\). The identity \(EJ=0\) implies \(JV=0\), but need not imply \(VJ=0\). If \(z\) is a weak-star cluster point of \(d(e_i)\) for a contractive approximate identity of \(J\), then \(d(j)=zj\) for every \(j\in J\). With \(\operatorname{ad}_z(b)=bz-zb\), the corrected derivation \(d_0=d+\operatorname{ad}_z\) vanishes on \(J\). The identity \(d_0(b)j=d_0(bj)=0\) puts its range in \(V_0=(E/\overline{\operatorname{span}}JE)^*\). Thus it descends as a bounded derivation \(Q\to V_0\). Conversely, an implementer in \(V\) of the pullback of a \(Q\)-derivation into \(V_0\) must lie in \(V_0\), since \(0=\operatorname{ad}_t(j)=-tj\). In particular pullback gives an isomorphism \(H^1(Q,V_0)\cong H^1(B,V)\).

If \(\Gamma\) is amenable and \(F\ne\varnothing\), the commuting unitaries from \(\mathcal U(C(F))\) and \(\Gamma\) give a representation of their amenable discrete direct product with dense linear span in \(Q\). For a unital dual coefficient on both sides, average the bounded cocycle \(h\mapsto d(U_h)U_h^*\) against a left invariant mean to obtain an implementer. The commuting left and right actions of the unit split a general dual coefficient into four components. The ones with a zero action on a side have inner derivations by the derivation identity at \(1\). Hence \(Q\) is amenable as a Banach algebra. When \(F\) is empty, \(Q=0\).

For the converse, if \(x\in F\) use the quotient onto \(C=C_r^*(\Gamma)\) at \(x\). The bimodule \(\mathcal B(\ell^2\Gamma)/C\) pulled back to \(B\) is contractive and unital on both sides, and its right \(C(X)\)-action is scalar at \(x\). The vector functional \(\tau(T)=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle\) restricts to a trace on \(C\), so \(a\mapsto a\tau-\tau a\) takes values in the dual of this quotient module. If it is inner, subtracting its implementer from \(\tau\) gives a \(C\)-central functional with value \(1\) at \(1\). On the diagonal this yields a nonzero left invariant bounded finitely additive measure on \(\Gamma\); normalizing its total variation gives a left invariant mean. Thus for nonamenable \(\Gamma\) the derivation remains non-inner after pullback along the surjection.

The descent and this criterion were checked independently in writing, not formalized in Lean.


## The sharp exponent for the right annihilator

Replace the left side of the geometric inequality by \((\sum_i\|\xi f_i\|^p)^{1/p}\), keeping the same right side. For \(1\le p<2\) the common right annihilator is still \(J_\mu\), and the preceding derivation criterion is unchanged. For every finite \(p\ge2\), the common right annihilator is zero.

In the disjoint-corner test write \(\eta=\xi f\) and \(t=bh\). Applying the new inequality to \(k,1-k\), where \(k\) is the cutoff used previously, gives
\[
\varepsilon^p\|\eta t\|^p
 \le\big((1+\varepsilon^2\|t\|^2)^{p/2}-1\big)\|\eta\|^p
 \le \tfrac p2\varepsilon^2\|t\|^2\|\eta\|^p
 \qquad(1\le p<2).
\]
The exponent \(2-p>0\) again forces \(E(fbh)=0\). Fixed-point evaluation still detects the quotient, and the modules satisfying the original \(\ell^1\) bound are among those satisfying the \(\ell^p\) bound, so the same counterexample to innerness applies.

For \(p=2\), let \(\pi\) be a faithful unital representation of \(B_\mu\) and use \(S_2(H)\) with left and right multiplication by \(\pi(B_\mu)\). For arbitrary nonnegative \(f_i\), not necessarily with disjoint supports, \(\sum_i f_i^2\le(\sum_i f_i)^2\) gives
\[
\sum_i\|T\pi(f_i)\|_2^2\le \Big\|\sum_i f_i\Big\|_\infty^2\|T\|_2^2.
\]
The right action is faithful by testing on rank-one operators. For \(p>2\) use the same module and the scalar \(\ell^p\)-\(\ell^2\) inequality. In the two-point swap, \(B_\mu=M_2\) and \(P_1+\varepsilon e_{10}\) gives equality in the \(\ell^2\) estimate for the two coordinate projections. The off-diagonal corner acts nontrivially even though there are no common fixed points.

This is a classification of the common annihilator, not of innerness for all coefficients at or above \(p=2\). The threshold calculation was independently checked in writing, not in Lean.

## Innerness at the endpoint when there is an invariant probability

Suppose \(X\) has a \(\Gamma\)-invariant regular Borel probability. Then for both the full and reduced crossed products, every bounded derivation into the dual of every two-sided essential contractive bimodule satisfying the right \(\ell^2\) estimate is inner if and only if \(\Gamma\) is amenable. By monotonicity of the coefficient classes, the same holds for each finite \(p\ge2\) under this probability hypothesis. The amenable-group direction does not require the probability: it follows by averaging over the unitaries from the amenable discrete semidirect product \(\mathcal U(C(X))\rtimes\Gamma\), whose span is dense.

For the converse it suffices to work with the reduced crossed product \(B\). The invariant probability and the canonical expectation give a trace \(\tau\) on \(B\). Let \(K=L^2(B,\tau)\), \(\Omega=\widehat 1\), and let \(\pi\) be the left representation. Use the balanced Banach projective tensor product \(\mathcal T=\mathcal B(K)\widehat\otimes_B K\), balancing right multiplication by \(\pi(B)\) on the first factor against the left representation on the second. The exterior left action multiplies the first factor on the left, and the exterior right action is the tracial right multiplication on \(K\). Set \(j(T)=T\otimes_B\Omega\) and \(E=\mathcal T/\overline{j(\pi(B))}\). The map \(j\) is a bimodule map since the left and right actions on \(\Omega\) agree. The Hilbert-space estimate for a positive family \((f_i)\) passes through the projective tensor norm by the triangle inequality in \(\ell^2\). In each quotient step one lift works for all \(i\), so \(E\) has the right \(\ell^2\) estimate with constant \(1\).

The functional \(\nu(T\otimes_B\xi)=\langle T\xi,\Omega\rangle\) restricts along \(j\circ\pi\) to \(\tau\). Hence \(d(b)=b\nu-\nu b\) takes values in \(E^*\). If \(d\) were inner with implementer \(\eta\), then \((\nu-\eta)\circ j\) would be \(\pi(B)\)-central and normalized at \(1\). In the GNS representation the group-coordinate diagonal is an isometric copy of \(\ell^\infty(\Gamma)\), even when the probability is not fully supported: the subspaces for distinct group coordinates are orthogonal and each contains a unit vector \(\widehat{u_g}\). Conjugating by group unitaries shifts this diagonal by left translation. Taking normalized total variation on the diagonal gives a left invariant mean. The quotient from full to reduced preserves the resulting non-inner example.

For example, let \(F_2\times C_2\) act on two points by the second factor. There are no common fixed points, but at \(p=2\) this gives a non-inner derivation. In its reduced model \(M_2(C_r^*(F_2))\), the witness can also be constructed with first tensor factor \(M_2(\mathcal B(\ell^2F_2))\); restricting to a rank-one matrix corner recovers half the scalar witness, the factor coming from the normalized matrix trace.

The endpoint argument was independently checked in writing, not formalized in Lean. The next section removes the invariant-probability hypothesis with a different coefficient module.

## A single right-\(\ell^2\) derivation tests topological amenability

For an arbitrary discrete \(\Gamma\) and nonempty compact Hausdorff \(X\), with no invariant probability assumed, topological amenability of the action is equivalent to each of the following, for either crossed product: nuclearity; innerness of every bounded derivation into the dual of every two-sided essential contractive right-\(\ell^2\) bimodule; and innerness of the single derivation below. The same test works for every finite \(p\ge2\), since the \(\ell^2\) coefficient also satisfies the \(\ell^p\) estimate.

Let \(M=C(X)^{**}\), \(N=\ell^\infty(\Gamma,M)\), and \(S=\mathcal B(\ell^2\Gamma)\bar\otimes M\). Use the regular representation \(\pi(f)_{t,t}=\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f)\), \(u_g=\lambda_g\otimes1\). Write \(q:N\to U=N/\iota(M)\), where \(\iota\) is the constant inclusion, and \(i(m)=1\otimes m\). Set
\[
(\beta_ga)(t)=\alpha_g(a(g^{-1}t)),\qquad
(\gamma_g y)_{s,t}=\alpha_g(y_{sg,tg}),\qquad
d(a)_{s,t}=\mathbf1_{\{s=t\}}a(t^{-1}).
\]
The dual coefficient consists of bounded maps \(T:U\to S\) satisfying \(T(mx)=i(m)T(x)\) and \(T\bar\beta_g=\gamma_gT\). Its predual is the quotient of \(U\widehat\otimes_\pi S_*\) by the closed span of
\[
mx\otimes\omega-x\otimes(\omega\circ L_{i(m)}),\qquad
\bar\beta_gx\otimes\omega-x\otimes(\omega\circ\gamma_g).
\]
The bimodule action on the dual is pointwise multiplication. It passes to the quotient predual since \(i(M)\) is central and \(\gamma_g\) fixes the represented crossed product pointwise.

For a positive family \(F_j=\pi(f_j)\), the predual right action is \(\omega\mapsto\omega\circ L_{F_j}\). If \(\omega(y)=\varphi(yv)\) is a polar decomposition, Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\|\omega\circ L_{F_j}\|^2\le\|\omega\|\varphi(F_j^2)\). Summation and \(\sum F_j^2\le(\sum F_j)^2\) give the required estimate on \(S_*\). It passes through the projective norm by the triangle inequality in \(\ell^2\), and through the quotient by using one lift for all \(j\).

The derivation is \(\Delta_r(b)(q(a))=[b,d(a)]\). It is well defined because \(d\iota=i\); the two relations hold because \(d\) is \(M\)-linear and \(d\beta_g=\gamma_gd\). If \(\Delta_r=\operatorname{ad}_T\), then \(d(a)-T(q(a))\) commutes with the regular group unitaries, so all diagonal entries agree. Its \((e,e)\) entry gives a bounded unital \(M\)-linear equivariant map \(L:N\to M\). On selfadjoint elements let \(L_0=\operatorname{Re}L\). The order modulus \(V(x)=\sup_{-x\le y\le x}L_0(y)\), for \(x\ge0\), is additive by decomposition of order intervals. It is equivariant and \(M\)-linear by the order identities for positive invertibles and then continuity. Thus \(V(1)^{-1}V\) is a unital positive equivariant \(M\)-module retraction. Neither \(T\) nor this retraction is assumed normal. Conversely a retraction \(P\) of this kind gives the implementer \(T(q(a))=d(a)-i(P(a))\).

The retraction criterion is [Anantharaman-Delaroche (1987), Theorem 4.9 and Remark 4.10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01461725); see also [Ozawa–Suzuki (2021), Theorem 3.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-021-00699-2). Amenability of the action gives nuclearity for both crossed products and equality of their norms, by finite-support completely positive factorizations through matrices over \(C(X)\). See [Anantharaman-Delaroche (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005014). Nuclearity implies Banach amenability by [Haagerup (1983), Theorem 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01394319), hence innerness for all the stated coefficients. For the full crossed product, pull back \(\Delta_r\) and its coefficient along the canonical surjection; surjectivity preserves non-innerness.

For instance, on the free-group boundary \(X=\partial F_2\), the \(F_2\) action passes the test, whereas the \(F_2\times F_2\) action through its first factor does not: a retraction for the latter would give an invariant mean on the second factor. Neither action has an invariant probability.

This result was independently checked in writing. No Lean verification or priority claim is made.

## Matrix-valued coefficients: central versus all positive tests

Let \(A=M_n(C(X))\) carry an arbitrary action \(\alpha\) of the discrete group \(\Gamma\), and let \(B_\mu=A\rtimes_{\alpha,\mu}\Gamma\) be the full or reduced crossed product. In a unital contractive Banach \(B_\mu\)-bimodule \(E\), consider
\[
 \left(\sum_j\|\xi a_j\|^2\right)^{1/2}\le
       \left\|\sum_j a_j\right\|\,\|\xi\|
\]
for every finite family of positive contractions from either \(Z(A)\) or all of \(A\). The two resulting universal innerness predicates agree: for each \(\mu\), they are equivalent to topological amenability of the induced action on \(X\) and to nuclearity of \(B_\mu\). The two classes of admissible coefficients, however, need not agree.

**A common test.** Use the preceding regular-diagonal construction with \(M=A^{**}\), \(Z=Z(M)\), \(N=\ell^\infty(\Gamma,Z)\), and \(S=\mathcal B(\ell^2\Gamma)\bar\otimes M\). Form the quotient by the *central* constants and impose the two balancing relations from the preceding section over \(Z\), rather than over \(M\). The corresponding derivation is again \(\Delta_\alpha(b)(qF)=[b,d(F)]\). In the normal predual estimate, polar decomposition gives, for arbitrary \(b_j\in S\),
\[
 \left(\sum_j\|\omega\circ L_{b_j}\|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le \|\omega\|\left\|\sum_j b_jb_j^*\right\|^{1/2}.
\]
For positive \(a_j\), set \(c=\|\sum_j a_j\|\). Functional calculus gives \(\sum_j a_j^2\le c\sum_j a_j\le c^2 1\) without commutativity. The bound passes through the Banach projective norm and through the quotient using a common lift. Thus the test qualifies even for the all-positive condition, with constant \(1\).

An implementer \(T\) makes \(d(F)-T(qF)\) commute with the represented crossed product. Its identity-coordinate diagonal entry commutes with \(A\) and hence lies in \(Z\); commutation with the group unitaries makes all diagonal entries equal. As in the preceding section, taking the order modulus and normalizing gives a central equivariant retraction. Conversely such a retraction gives \(T(qF)=d(F)-1\otimes P(F)\). The center here is \(C(X)^{**}I_n\), with its induced action, so the retraction tests topological amenability even with nontrivial fiber automorphisms. See [Anantharaman-Delaroche (1987), Theorem 4.9 and Remark 4.10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01461725) and [Ozawa–Suzuki (2021), Theorem 3.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-021-00699-2). For an amenable center action, the finite-support completely positive factorizations use central coefficients and matrices over the nuclear algebra \(A\). They give nuclearity of both crossed products; [Haagerup (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01394319) then gives innerness for all contractive coefficients. In the reverse direction even the all-positive universal predicate implies innerness of the distinguished test.

For the standard amplification \(\alpha=\operatorname{id}_{M_n}\otimes\sigma\), this test is entrywise the scalar derivation. Scalar implementers amplify by \(I_n\otimes T\). Conversely an implementer must commute with the constant matrix units because the derivation vanishes on those units, so the \((1,1)\) corner recovers a scalar implementer with the same norm. This explicit correspondence is for the standard amplification, not arbitrary fiber actions.

**The coefficient class is not recovered by taking corners.** For \(A=M_2\) and \(X\) a point, the center condition is automatic. The regular bimodule with operator norm fails the all-positive condition: take \(\xi=1\), \(a_1=e_{11}\), \(a_2=e_{22}\), giving \(\sqrt2>1\). With Hilbert–Schmidt norm the same matrix space satisfies the all-positive condition by the estimate on \(\sum_j a_j^2\).

A stronger obstruction uses \(D=C(\mathbb T)\), the crossed product of the trivial \(\mathbb Z\)-action on a point. Its regular bimodule with the supremum norm qualifies on the scalar side. In any all-positive coefficient for the standard amplification \(M_2(D)\), let \(p=e_{11}\) and \(y\in pEp\). Apply the estimate for \(e_{11},e_{22}\) to \(y(e_{11}b+e_{12}c)\). Matrix units identify the two right corners isometrically, giving
\[
 \|yb\|^2+\|yc\|^2\le\|y\|^2\|bb^*+cc^*\|.
\]
Induction with \(h=(\sum_{i<m}b_i b_i^*+\varepsilon1)^{1/2}\) and \(h^{-1}b_i\), then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), gives the same estimate for any finite row. A right module embedding \(J:D\to pEp\) bounded below by \(c_0>0\) would therefore satisfy \(m c_0^2\le\|J1\|^2\), using \(m\) disjointly supported positive norm-one functions. Thus even allowing equivalent norms does not make this scalar coefficient obtainable by the standard corner functor. The result is about that functor, not a claim about every possible equivalence of categories.

This extension was independently checked in writing. No Lean verification, priority claim, or general nuclearity characterization for arbitrary noncommutative coefficient algebras is made.


## Locally trivial matrix bundles

The preceding equivalence holds as well for the section algebra \(A\) of a locally trivial bundle of unital full matrix algebras over nonempty compact Hausdorff \(X\). The matrix size may be locally constant and need not be the same on all of \(X\). Let a discrete group act by arbitrary automorphisms of \(A\), inducing its action on \(X\). For each of the full and reduced crossed products, the universal innerness tests with positive elements of \(Z(A)\) or all positive elements of \(A\), innerness of the same distinguished derivation, topological amenability of the base action, and nuclearity of the crossed product are equivalent. The coefficient condition is right-sided, for arbitrary finite positive families, not just orthogonal ones. The distinguished coefficient has a unital contractive predual bimodule.

**Identifying the center.** Let \(c:C(X)\to A\) be scalar inclusion and let \(t:A\to C(X)\) take the normalized fiber trace. Choose a finite trivializing cover and a subordinate partition of unity \((\rho_j)\). In a chart of rank \(n_j\), multiply each local matrix unit by \(\sqrt{\rho_j/n_j}\) and extend by zero, giving a finite family \((w_k)\). Then
\[
c(t(a))=\sum_k w_k a w_k^*,\qquad \sum_k w_k w_k^*=1.
\]
This equality extends normally to \(A^{**}\). Applied to \(z\in Z(A^{**})\), it gives \(c^{**}t^{**}(z)=z\). Since \(t c=\mathrm{id}\) and \(c^{**}\) has central image, \(c^{**}\) identifies \(C(X)^{**}\) with \(Z(A^{**})\). This identification is equivariant: it is induced by scalar inclusion and needs no equivariant choice of chart.

**Transporting the test.** In the regular construction above, use \(M=A^{**}\), with the same balancing relations over \(Z(M)\) and the same covariance relations in the predual quotient. The commutator derivation and the compression at the identity group index are unchanged; after compression, balancing and commutation with \(A\) place the value in \(M\cap A'=Z(M)\). The all-positive estimate again follows from \(\sum_j a_j^2\le\|\sum_j a_j\|\sum_j a_j\) and uses one common lift in the quotient. Neither argument uses global matrix units. In the full case the test is pulled back along the surjection onto the reduced crossed product, which also reflects innerness of that test.

The resulting positive equivariant expectation on the center gives topological amenability by [Ozawa–Suzuki (2021), Theorem 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00029-021-00699-2) applied to \(C(X)\). In this commutative case their quasi-central approximation property is the Reiter criterion: taking pointwise square roots of probability vectors converts \(\ell^1\) defects to squared \(\ell^2\) defects, and taking squared absolute values and normalizing converts back by Cauchy–Schwarz. The section algebra itself is nuclear, by local matrix evaluations, finite covers and a partition of unity giving completely positive point-norm approximations. [Anantharaman–Delaroche (2002), Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005014) gives nuclearity and full-to-reduced equality for an amenable base action with this nuclear coefficient algebra. [Haagerup (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01394319) then gives innerness for all contractive coefficients.

**A global matrix corner need not exist.** Glue a complex rank-two bundle \(V\) over \(S^4\) along the equator by the degree-one map \(S^3\cong SU(2)\). This clutching class remains nonzero in \(\pi_3(U(2))\). A continuous rank-one projection in \(\operatorname{End}(V)\) would split \(V\) into two complex line bundles, both trivial on \(S^4\), a contradiction. The group-index compression used by the derivation is not a rank-one corner in the matrix bundle and still works. This does not obstruct ordinary Morita equivalence of \(\operatorname{End}(V)\) and \(C(S^4)\).
