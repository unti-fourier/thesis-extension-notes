# Which part of a crossed product acts on right-\(\ell^1\) coefficients?

**Work in progress, November 2026.** Research notes toward a possible future paper. This is not a submission, a peer-reviewed publication, or a publication-list entry. The proof has been independently checked in writing; no Lean verification or priority claim is made.

Let a discrete group \(\Gamma\) act on a nonempty compact Hausdorff space \(X\). Write \(B_\mu=C(X)\rtimes_\mu\Gamma\) for either the full or reduced crossed product and \(F=X^\Gamma\) for the common fixed-point set. Consider contractive Banach \(B_\mu\)-bimodules \(E\) satisfying
\[
\sum_i\|\xi f_i\|\le \|\xi\|\,\Big\|\sum_i f_i\Big\|_\infty
\]
for nonnegative \(f_i\in C(X)\) and \(\xi\in E\). No unitality assumption on \(E\) is needed.

**Claim.** The common right annihilator of these modules is the closed two-sided ideal \(J_\mu\) generated by \(C_0(X\setminus F)\), and
\[
B_\mu/J_\mu\cong C(F,C^*_\mu(\Gamma)).
\]
In particular, without a common fixed point all these right module actions are zero. The reduced statement does not require exactness of \(\Gamma\).

**Disjoint corners.** For \(f,h\) with disjoint supports choose \(0\le p\le1\) with \(fp=f\), \(ph=0\). Set \(\eta=\xi f\) and \(r=p+\varepsilon bh\) for \(b\in B_\mu\). Then \(\eta rp=\eta\), \(\eta r(1-p)=\varepsilon\xi fbh\), and \(rr^*=p^2+\varepsilon^2bhh^*b^*\). Thus
\[
\varepsilon\|\xi fbh\|
 \le \big(\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2\|bh\|^2}-1\big)\|\eta\|.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives \(E(fbh)=0\). Continuous cutoffs followed by norm limits give the same conclusion when \(fh=0\). If \(gx\ne x\), choose \(h\in C(X)\) nonzero at \(x\) and supported in a neighborhood disjoint from its \(g\)-translate. There is \(f\) with \(fh=0\) and \(fu_gh=u_gh\). Thus \(Eu_gh=0\) and \(Eh=0\), since the right annihilator is a two-sided ideal. Such \(h\) generate \(C_0(X\setminus F)\).

**Identifying the quotient.** At \(x\in F\), evaluation sends \(f\) to \(f(x)1\) and \(u_g\) to the group unitary. In the reduced case this is a component of the regular representation built from the direct sum of all point evaluations. Taken together, these maps give \(B_\mu/J_\mu\to C(F,C_\mu^*(\Gamma))\). Conversely, in the quotient the images of \(C(F)\) and the canonical \(C_\mu^*(\Gamma)\) commute and generate it. Their product gives a map from the maximal tensor product, which is the spatial tensor product because \(C(F)\) is nuclear. Fixed-point evaluation is inverse on elementary tensors, and the product map is onto. Each fixed point then gives a contractive bimodule by pulling back the regular multiplication module of \(C_\mu^*(\Gamma)\); the right \(C(X)\)-action is scalar there. These modules detect every nonzero element of the quotient. If \(F=\varnothing\), the ideal contains \(1\) and the quotient is zero.

**Contrast with the row-norm algebra.** This collapse does not carry to \(A_0(\Gamma,X)\) with its row norm, as in [Awazu (2025), Definitions 2.3 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08357). On its regular dual, \((\varphi\cdot a)(c)=\varphi(ac)\). Left multiplication by \(f_i\) acts on every coefficient by the same function \(f_i\); consequently \(\|\sum_i f_i a_i\|_{\rm row}\le\|\sum_i f_i\|_\infty\) for \(\|a_i\|_{\rm row}\le1\). Choosing scalar phases independently proves the right-\(\ell^1\) estimate. Evaluating \(\varphi\cdot a\) at \(1\) detects every nonzero \(a\), so the common right annihilator for the row-norm algebra is zero.

## Consequence for derivations

For either crossed product, every bounded derivation into the dual of every such coefficient module is inner if and only if the fixed-point quotient \(Q=B_\mu/J_\mu\) is amenable as a Banach algebra. These statements are equivalent to \(F=\varnothing\) or \(\Gamma\) being amenable. The criterion is unchanged if one tests only bimodules essential on both sides.

To see the descent, write \(B=B_\mu\), \(J=J_\mu\), and \(V=E^*\). The identity \(EJ=0\) implies \(JV=0\), but need not imply \(VJ=0\). If \(z\) is a weak-star cluster point of \(d(e_i)\) for a contractive approximate identity of \(J\), then \(d(j)=zj\) for every \(j\in J\). With \(\operatorname{ad}_z(b)=bz-zb\), the corrected derivation \(d_0=d+\operatorname{ad}_z\) vanishes on \(J\). The identity \(d_0(b)j=d_0(bj)=0\) puts its range in \(V_0=(E/\overline{\operatorname{span}}JE)^*\). Thus it descends as a bounded derivation \(Q\to V_0\). Conversely, an implementer in \(V\) of the pullback of a \(Q\)-derivation into \(V_0\) must lie in \(V_0\), since \(0=\operatorname{ad}_t(j)=-tj\). In particular pullback gives an isomorphism \(H^1(Q,V_0)\cong H^1(B,V)\).

If \(\Gamma\) is amenable and \(F\ne\varnothing\), the commuting unitaries from \(\mathcal U(C(F))\) and \(\Gamma\) give a representation of their amenable discrete direct product with dense linear span in \(Q\). For a unital dual coefficient on both sides, average the bounded cocycle \(h\mapsto d(U_h)U_h^*\) against a left invariant mean to obtain an implementer. The commuting left and right actions of the unit split a general dual coefficient into four components. The ones with a zero action on a side have inner derivations by the derivation identity at \(1\). Hence \(Q\) is amenable as a Banach algebra. When \(F\) is empty, \(Q=0\).

For the converse, if \(x\in F\) use the quotient onto \(C=C_r^*(\Gamma)\) at \(x\). The bimodule \(\mathcal B(\ell^2\Gamma)/C\) pulled back to \(B\) is contractive and unital on both sides, and its right \(C(X)\)-action is scalar at \(x\). The vector functional \(\tau(T)=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle\) restricts to a trace on \(C\), so \(a\mapsto a\tau-\tau a\) takes values in the dual of this quotient module. If it is inner, subtracting its implementer from \(\tau\) gives a \(C\)-central functional with value \(1\) at \(1\). On the diagonal this yields a nonzero left invariant bounded finitely additive measure on \(\Gamma\); normalizing its total variation gives a left invariant mean. Thus for nonamenable \(\Gamma\) the derivation remains non-inner after pullback along the surjection.

The descent and this criterion were checked independently in writing, not formalized in Lean.
