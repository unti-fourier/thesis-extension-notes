# Ordinary Banach amenability of the two-sided étale groupoid algebra

**Research note in progress (2026-11-26).** Direction 6. Written proof checked; not Lean-formalized. Not a paper, a preprint, or a peer-reviewed publication. No priority or optimal-constant claim.

For a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid \(\mathcal G\) with counting Haar system, let \(A_I\) denote the untwisted complex completion of \(C_c(\mathcal G)\) in the maximum of the two absolute fibre-sum norms. Then \(A_I\) is Banach amenable if and only if \(\mathcal G\) is topologically amenable. In the amenable case the ordinary approximate diagonal can have bound arbitrarily close to \(2\). This extends the [ample construction](06-ample-raw-i-amenability.md), whose final section already proves the general converse. The forward implication below does not assume second countability or that the unit space is zero-dimensional.

### A uniform bisection decomposition bound

Write \(\beta(f)\) for the infimum of \(\sum_j\|f_j\|_\infty\) over finite decompositions into functions compactly supported in open bisections. This is the norm \(\|\cdot\|_{\max}\) of [Bardadyn–Kwaśniewski–McKee, v4, Lemma 4.5 and Remark 4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09997v4#S4.SS1) in the scalar case. They record \(\|f\|_I\le\beta(f)\). For the upper bound, scale \(f\) to \(\|f\|_I=1\) and test with any \(\beta\)-dual functional of norm one. On a relatively compact neighbourhood \(W\) of the support, finite bisection partitions of unity bound its restriction in sup norm, yielding a complex Radon measure \(\mu\). The variation is at most one on each open bisection in \(W\). The same holds on a Borel bisection in \(W\): each of its compact subsets is contained in an open bisection, by compactness and the closedness of the off-diagonal range- or source-collision relations. These relations are closed because local injectivity isolates the diagonal within each equality relation.

Replicate each arrow \(\lfloor N|f|\rfloor\) times and greedily colour the copies in \(2N-1\) colours using a finite Borel disjoint refinement of a bisection cover of the support. At either endpoint at most \(N-1\) previously coloured copies can interfere. The available colours vary in a Borel manner, because endpoint images within each chart are Borel under its homeomorphisms. Each colour carries variation at most one, so \(\int\lfloor N|f|\rfloor\,d|\mu|\le 2N-1\). Dividing by \(N\) and passing to the limit, then taking the supremum over dual functionals, gives
\[
 \|f\|_I\le\beta(f)\le2\|f\|_I .
\]

### Continuous transport

First let the unit space \(X\) be compact. Denote range and source sums by \(R,C\). For \(u\in\mathcal U=\{u\in C_c(\mathcal G)_+:Ru=1\}\), put \(T_u h=C((h\circ r)u)\); thus \(T_{a*u}=T_uT_a\). The transport identity of the [ample note](06-ample-raw-i-amenability.md#two-sided-transport) remains valid. In its separation argument replace clopen pasting by the following. Fix a probability \(\mu\) and set \(\lambda_u(h)=\mu(T_u h)\), \(L(h)=\inf_u\lambda_u(h)\) for \(h\ge0\). To compare \(L(h+k)\) with \(L(h)+L(k)\), choose \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) near-optimal for \(h,k\), and set \(\rho=\lambda_1+\lambda_2\). A continuous \(c:X\to[0,1]\) can approximate the indicator of the set \(d\lambda_1/d\rho\le d\lambda_2/d\rho\) in \(L^1(\rho)\), by regularity. Pasting on the range side with \(c,1-c\) gives \(\lambda_u=c\lambda_1+(1-c)\lambda_2\), arbitrarily close in variation to the pointwise minimum of their densities times \(\rho\). Its value on \(h+k\) is therefore at most \(\lambda_1(h)+\lambda_2(k)\) up to arbitrarily small error. This proves additivity together with the reverse inequality for an infimum. Thus \(L\) is integration against a positive measure \(\nu\le\mu\).

For a bisection \(B\) and nonnegative \(h\in C_c(r(B))\), use a cutoff \(k\in C_c(B)\) in \([0,1]\) equal to one over \(\operatorname{supp}h\). Then \(a=k+(1-Rk)1_X\) belongs to \(\mathcal U\), and \(T_a h\) is exactly the transport of \(h\) along the inverse of the bisection, extended by zero. Since \(\nu(h)\le\nu(T_a h)\) by composition, the inverse bisection proves equality. Thus \(\nu\) is invariant. The earlier separation argument now applies without clopen partitions, giving \(\inf_u\|T_uw\|_\infty=\sup(\{0\}\cup\{\sigma(w):\sigma\text{ invariant probability}\})\) for \(w\ge0\).

For tests \(b_j\) compactly supported in open bisections use \(\Delta_b f=b*f-((Rb)\circ r)f\). Start with \(f\ge0\), \(Rf=1\), and small \(\|R|\Delta_{b_j} f|\|_\infty\) from Reiter, using compact cutoffs and filling missing mass at units. Apply the transport identity to \(w=Cf+M\sum_j C|\Delta_{b_j}f|\), as in the ample case. Right convolution by a chosen \(u\in\mathcal U\) commutes with \(\Delta_b\), preserves row mass, and transports the absolute column defects. This gives \(p=f*u\) with \(Rp=1\), \(\|p\|_I<1+\varepsilon\) and \(\|\Delta_{b_j}p\|_I<\varepsilon\) for finitely many prescribed tests and arbitrary \(\varepsilon>0\).

### Tensor construction

On the algebraic tensor balanced over \(C(X)\), an elementary tensor \(a\otimes d\) is represented by \(a(\alpha)d(\beta)\) on composable pairs. The representation is injective on the compactly supported algebraic core: on a bisection-pair chart, composable pairs are parameterized by the middle unit \(y=s\alpha=r\beta\). A compactly supported function there has a local inverse \(H\mapsto (\ell H)\otimes k\) using cutoffs \(\ell,k\) equal to one over its support in the two charts. A smaller middle-unit cutoff shows independence of the choice by balancing. On intersections the cutoffs may be taken in the intersection. Finite partitions of unity on the composable-pair space subordinate to simultaneous refinements of the finitely many local supports glue these inverses; on a local elementary tensor the left and right scalar coefficients multiply by balancing, so the glued map is a left inverse.

Define \(J(p)\) using the function \(p(\alpha)1_X(\alpha\beta)\) on composable pairs. For \(p\) supported in a bisection it is \(p\otimes v^\ast\), with a cutoff \(v\) in the same bisection equal to one near \(\operatorname{supp}p\). Thus \(\|J(p)\|\le\beta(p)\le2\|p\|_I\) in balanced projective norm. Multiplication gives \(Rp\). For a test \(b\) supported in an open bisection and cutoff \(v\) equal to one near \(\operatorname{supp}b\), checking the two composable coordinates gives
\[
 bJ(p)-J(p)b=J(\Delta_b p)v.
\]
Both sides at \((\alpha,\beta)\) are \(b(\alpha\beta)[p(\beta^{-1})-p(\alpha)]\). These identities only use the algebraic injectivity, not injectivity of the completed projective tensor representation.

Average lifts to the ordinary projective product by \(a\otimes d\mapsto |F|^{-1}\sum_{v\in F}av\otimes\bar v d\), where \(F\) runs through Følner sets of the discrete abelian group of unitary elements of \(C(X)\). These are contractions preserving multiplication and commuting with the outer actions. They tend to zero on the balancing kernel, first for a difference involving a single unitary by the Følner property, then by linear span and closure. As in the [ample argument](06-ample-raw-i-amenability.md#diagonal-and-norm-estimate), lift \(J(p)\) nearly isometrically, then use one common average on the finitely many balanced-small commutators. This gives ordinary approximate diagonals with bound arbitrarily close to \(2\).

For noncompact \(X\), adjoin a single isolated orbit at infinity by replacing just the clopen unit component by \(X^+\). A compact nonunit test has compact endpoint sets in \(X\). Cut the original continuous Reiter field by compactly supported continuous cutoffs retaining mass \(1-\eta\) at these endpoints and fill missing mass at the units. The defect increases by at most \(4\eta\) and is zero on unit arrows, giving amenability of the enlargement. Compress its ordinary diagonal by \(a\otimes d\mapsto ea\otimes de\), where the unit cutoff \(0\le e\le1\) is compactly supported and equals one on all endpoints of the finite tests. This is contractive and lands in the original product. Its multiplication \(e^2\) acts as identity on those tests, and the same commutator estimates hold. Density concludes. Nothing here gives a propagation bound for the transport kernel.
