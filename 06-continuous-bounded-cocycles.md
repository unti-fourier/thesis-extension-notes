# Topological amenability and approximate continuous primitives

**Work in progress, 2026-11-26.** A written-checked research note, not a paper or preprint. Not verified in Lean. No priority claim.

Let \(G\rightrightarrows X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff groupoid with a full continuous left Haar system. The compact-open norm topology for a cocycle means uniform convergence in the fiber norm on each compact set of arrows. In this topology, topological amenability is equivalent to an approximate first-cohomology criterion. Exact vanishing with continuous primitives fails even for a compact-unit ample groupoid.

## The criterion

Write \(A_x=L^1(G^x,\lambda^x;\mathbb R)\), \(I_x(a)=\int a\,d\lambda^x\), and \(V_x=\ker I_x\). Assume there is a positive continuous \(L^1\) section \(p\) with \(I_x(p_x)=1\) for all \(x\). This holds if \(X\) is paracompact, or if \(G\) is étale with counting Haar system, in which case \(p_x=\delta_{1_x}\).

The following are equivalent.

1. \(G\) is topologically amenable.
2. For any continuous Banach bundle with enough local continuous sections and a continuous linear isometric action \(U\) of \(G\), every bounded norm-continuous \(1\)-cocycle \(c\) is a compact-open norm limit of coboundaries \(\partial v_i(\gamma)=v_i(r\gamma)-U_\gamma v_i(s\gamma)\) of continuous sections with \(\sup_i\|v_i\|_\infty\le\|c\|_\infty\).
3. The single bounded continuous \(V\)-valued cocycle \(b_p(\gamma)=p_{r\gamma}-L_\gamma p_{s\gamma}\), where \(L_\gamma\) is left translation, is a compact-open norm limit of coboundaries of continuous \(V\)-sections. No uniform bound on these sections is required.

For (1) to (2), take continuous Reiter probability fields \(m_i^x\) and define \(v_i(x)=\int_{G^x}c(h)\,dm_i^x(h)\). The integrals are Bochner integrals: each Radon probability is carried by a countable union of compact sets, on which the image in the fixed fiber is separable. A compact cutoff capturing all but \(\varepsilon\) of the mass at \(x\) does so, with an arbitrarily small additional error, near \(x\). On that compact support, local continuous sections and a finite partition of unity approximate \(c(h)\) in norm by finite sums of sections at \(rh\) multiplied by scalar functions of \(h\). These sums integrate to continuous sections by continuity of the probability fields. The cocycle identity gives
\[
 \|\partial v_i(\gamma)-c(\gamma)\|
 \le \|c\|_\infty\|m_i^{r\gamma}-\gamma_*m_i^{s\gamma}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The probability mass also gives \(\|v_i\|_\infty\le\|c\|_\infty\). For the Reiter definition, see [Renault (2013), Definition 2.6 and the comparison immediately following it](https://arxiv.org/html/1302.0636#S2).

The \(L^1\) bundle used in (3) is generated by \(f_x=f|_{G^x}\) for \(f\in C_c(G)\). Their norms are continuous by the Haar property. Compactly supported continuous functions on a row extend to compactly supported continuous functions on \(G\): choose a compact neighborhood of the support and extend on that compact Hausdorff space before applying a cutoff. A cutoff on \(X\) then makes the supremum of the row norms at most the original row norm plus any prescribed positive error. Extending the summable differences of a rapidly convergent row approximation produces a section through each prescribed fiber element.

Left translation preserves \(I_x\) and is isometric. To see continuity, test against generating sections \(f,h\) by taking the row integral of \(|f(\gamma^{-1}\eta)-h(\eta)|\). With \(\gamma\) in a compact neighborhood \(P\), the \(\eta\)-support is contained in \(P\operatorname{supp}f\cup\operatorname{supp}h\). On the resulting compact set of pairs with equal range, approximate the continuous integrand uniformly by sums of products of continuous functions in the two variables, inserting a common compact cutoff in \(\eta\). The row measures of that fixed compact support are uniformly bounded by the Haar property. This proves continuity on generating sections, and the isometry gives joint continuity.

When \(X\) is paracompact, use nonnegative \(C_c(G)\) functions with positive row integral locally, normalize there, and patch with a locally finite partition of unity on \(X\). The full-support condition gives the initial functions. For counting Haar on an étale groupoid the unit section is locally a generating section after a cutoff on \(X\). The continuous projection \(a\mapsto a-I_x(a)p_x\) gives enough sections of \(V\). These observations justify applying (2) to \(b_p\), which has norm at most \(2\).

For (3) to (1), let \(u_x=p_x-v(x)\), so \(I_x(u_x)=1\), and set \(q_x=|u_x|/\|u_x\|_1\). Fiberwise absolute value is \(1\)-Lipschitz and takes generating sections to generating sections, hence is continuous on the bundle. Because \(\|u_x\|_1\ge1\),
\[
 \|q_{r\gamma}-L_\gamma q_{s\gamma}\|_1
 \le 2\|u_{r\gamma}-L_\gamma u_{s\gamma}\|_1
 =2\|b_p(\gamma)-\partial v(\gamma)\|_1.
\]
The measures \(q_x\lambda^x\) form a continuous probability field: for each scalar \(a\in C_c(G)\), approximate \(q_x\) locally in row norm by a generating section \(f_x\) and use continuity of \(\lambda^x(af)\). The displayed estimate is a Reiter estimate. No common bound on \(v\) is needed.

The single test in (3) is a cohomological form of the Reiter condition. For compact actions of countable discrete groups, the Dirac difference is also used, up to sign, by [Brodzki, Niblo, Nowak and Wright (2010 preprint), Definition 18 and Theorem B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0295). Their criterion uses exact vanishing in bidual coefficients. The criterion above uses approximation within the original continuous bundle, and makes no assertion about exact vanishing in that bundle.

## Exact continuous primitives can fail

Let \(X\) be the one-point compactification of the discrete disjoint union of cycles \(O_k=\mathbb Z/(2k)\), \(k\ge 1\). Let \(T\) advance one step on each cycle and fix \(\infty\). The transformation groupoid \(\mathbb Z\ltimes X\) is ample and topologically amenable, as seen by placing the same uniform probability on the labels \([-N,N]\) of each range row.

Define \(F(\infty)=0\) and \(F(k,j)=\min(j,2k-j)/k\) for \(0\le j<2k\). With trivial real coefficients, \(b(n,x)=F(T^n x)-F(x)\) is a \(1\)-cocycle of norm \(1\). It is continuous because for each fixed \(n\), its magnitude on \(O_k\) is at most \(|n|/k\). Any continuous primitive \(v\) would differ from \(F\) by a constant on each cycle, hence \(v(k,k)-v(k,0)=1\). Both sequences tend to \(\infty\), contradicting continuity.

Truncating \(F\) to the first \(M\) cycles gives continuous primitives of norm at most \(1\) for approximating coboundaries. On every compact set of arrows the labels \(n\) lie in a finite interval \([-L,L]\); the error is at most \(L/(M+1)\). In contrast, the uniform error over all arrows is at least \(1\) for the coboundary of any continuous \(v\): test the half-turn \((k,(k,0))\) and let \(k\) tend to infinity. The function \(F\) itself is a bounded Borel primitive, so the example does not obstruct measured vanishing. Nor does it obstruct dual-coefficient innerness: for example \(F\) defines an element of \(C(X)^{**}\) by integration against finite measures.

For a circle-action example of the failure of exact continuity, see [Coronel, Navas and Ponce (2011 preprint), Example 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3523#page=8). The exact real-coefficient theorem under minimality is given by [Renault (2011 preprint), Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.4322#page=2).
