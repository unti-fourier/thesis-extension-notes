# Compact pair groupoids with finitely many point atoms

**Work in progress.** Written verification only. Not a paper, Lean formalization, or priority claim.

Let \(X\) be compact Hausdorff and \(\mu\) a full-support Radon probability. Suppose it has finitely many point atoms of masses \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\), and the remaining measure is nonatomic of mass \(d=1-\sum_a p_a>0\). The raw \(I\)-norm completion \(A\) of continuous pair-groupoid kernels has ordinary Banach amenability constant
\[
\operatorname{AM}(A)=d^{-1}.
\]
The atoms need not be isolated, and the nonatomic part need not itself have full support.

**Lower bound.** In the Schur representation \(T\) on \(L^2(\mu)\) let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto the atomic subspace. The map \(a\otimes b\mapsto T_aPT_b\) has trace-norm bound \(r\|a\|_I\|b\|_I\). For a bounded approximate diagonal \(m\), the resulting trace-class operator \(S_m\) asymptotically commutes with every continuous rank-one kernel. Using arbitrarily large continuous orthonormal systems containing \(1\), the diagonal argument in the [single-atom note](06-pair-groupoid-one-isolated-atom.md) gives \(\langle S_m1,1\rangle\to0\). On the other hand \(\langle T_{\pi(m)}1,1\rangle\to1\). The difference is the contribution through the nonatomic part. Its bilinear norm is at most \(d\), by integrating \(|(T_a^*1)(y)(T_b1)(y)|\) over that part and using the two \(I\)-bounds. Thus the approximate-diagonal bound is at least \(d^{-1}\). No discontinuous indicator is used as an algebra element in this argument.

**Weighted continuous partition.** Choose a fine finite open cover for the test kernels in both variables. Use the singletons of the atoms as compact cores and choose \(N\) diffuse compact cores as in the [nonatomic construction](06-pair-groupoid-raw-i-amenability.md), with target weights \(w_i=p_a\) for atoms and \(w_i=d/N\) for diffuse pieces. More precisely, refine the cover by open sets with closures inside the original opens, assign a Borel partition, subtract the atoms, and round the relative nonatomic masses of the pieces to numbers of labels summing to \(N\). Positive masses can be divided equally among their assigned labels so each target is reached within relative error \(\varepsilon/2\). Choose cores by inner regularity, with total missing mass less than \((\varepsilon/2)\min_i w_i\); the singleton cores are included intact. The closures of positive-mass pieces together with the atomic points cover \(X\) by full support, and the assigned original opens therefore cover \(X\). Pairwise disjoint cutoffs on the cores, plus a subordinate partition for their leftover, give continuous weights \(v_i\) with \(\int v_i\le(1+\varepsilon)w_i\) and \(\int v_i^2\ge(1-\varepsilon)w_i\). Put \(h_i=(v_i-\int v_i)/(1+\varepsilon)+w_i\). These nonnegative weights integrate exactly to \(w_i\), sum to one, and differ from \(v_i\) by at most \(2\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)\) in pointwise \(\ell^1\).

Use the weighted matrix norm from the single-atom note and the map \(B\mapsto T(B)(x,y)=\sum_{ij}h_i(x)B_{ij}h_j(y)/w_j\). This is contractive and multiplication is twisted by \(G_{jk}=\int h_jh_k/w_j\). Row sums are one and \(w_jG_{jk}=w_kG_{kj}\). Thus the two components of the weighted norm of \(G-I\) coincide and \(\|G-I\|_w=2\max_i(1-G_{ii})\). Since \(h_i\ge v_i/(1+\varepsilon)\), this is at most \(2(1-(1-\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)^2)\), independent of \(N\). Interpolation using \(B_{ij}=w_j f(x_i,x_j)\) approximates each test kernel uniformly with \(\|B\|_w\le\|f\|_\infty\).

**Weighted matrix diagonal.** Use the exact finite matrix diagonal with coefficient \(1/N\) on every \(E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}\) whose intermediate index \(j\) is diffuse, and zero on the other intermediate indices. Assign disjoint uniformly random sets of \(k_a=\lfloor Np_a\rfloor\) diffuse columns to the atomic rows, so each \(k_a\) is positive for large \(N\). Injectively match the remaining \(N-\sum_a k_a\) columns to uniformly chosen distinct diffuse rows. For atomic row \(a\), the block entries in the two factors are \(1/k_a\) and \(1\) along each pair and its reverse. Reciprocal rescaling balances the two norms of that block at \(\sqrt{Np_a/(dk_a)}\). For a matched diffuse pair each entry is \(\sqrt{N/(N-\sum_a k_a)}\). The different blocks have disjoint row and column supports in each factor. Averaging shared independent signs for each pair eliminates cross-terms. The sampling probabilities times the two-factor products give coefficient \(1/N\) in both kinds of entries. Hence the diagonal norm is bounded by
\[
\max\left\{\frac{N}{N-\sum_a k_a},\ \max_a\frac{Np_a}{dk_a}\right\}\longrightarrow \frac1d .
\]
Transport this diagonal by \(T\otimes T\); the difference from exact matrix centrality and the error in its product are controlled by \(\|G-I\|_w\) times bounded factors as in the single-atom note. Uniform kernel approximation on finite test families gives the desired approximate diagonal with bounds arbitrarily close to \(d^{-1}\). This proves the equality. The case with no atoms was covered in the nonatomic construction.

No conclusion for infinitely many atoms or for non-pair groupoids is asserted.
